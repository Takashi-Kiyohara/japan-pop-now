## What Is the My Hero Academia Waffle Diner?

DECOTTO is a regular Ikebukuro sandwich diner that licenses short anime runs between normal service months. From **April 3 through April 26, 2026** the entire menu is replaced with a 10th-anniversary My Hero Academia set — three pressed waffle sandwiches built around Deku, Bakugo, and Todoroki, plus themed sodas and a dessert waffle.

The 10th-anniversary framing matters. Weekly Shonen Jump ran the manga across its full serialization arc, and Shueisha and TOHO are rolling anniversary collaborations across 2026, with the Ikebukuro diner positioned as the casual Tokyo anchor. Osaka and Nagoya pop-ups are scheduled for summer — different venues, different menus — but DECOTTO has the only waffle-format run this year.

Walking in, the shop feels small: about 18 counter seats and 12 table seats across roughly 50 square metres. The anime takeover is limited to menu, wall displays, table coasters, and drink-drip trays. There is no IP dressing on the windows and no queue-line character cutout, which keeps the vibe more "themed lunch" than "cosplay photo spot."

## What Are the Waffle Sandwiches?

Three pressed waffle sandwiches, each plated with character colour cues and served with one random-draw coaster from a 9-design set. Every character has three coaster variants (A, B, C), so one repeat order does not guarantee a repeat character.

| Waffle Sandwich | Build | Price |
|---|---|---|
| **Deku (One For All)** | Green matcha waffle, grilled chicken + avocado + honey mustard, micro-greens | **1,380 yen** |
| **Bakugo (Explosion)** | Charcoal-black waffle, spicy beef + jalapeno + smoked cheddar, chili crema | **1,480 yen** |
| **Todoroki (Half-Cold Half-Hot)** | Half-white buttermilk / half-charred waffle, salmon + beef duo, wasabi mayo + barbecue glaze | **1,480 yen** |

## How Do I Book? (Walk-in vs TableCheck)

There is no lottery. DECOTTO is straightforward compared to ufotable or BOX cafe — you can walk in, you can book online, either works.

**Walk-in (zero cost, queue risk):**
- Weekday lunch (12:00 — 14:00): 10-20 minute wait
- Weekday evening (17:00 — 19:00): 20-30 minute wait
- Saturday all day: 40-50 minutes peak, 20 minutes if you arrive right at 11:00 open
- **Sunday April 26 (final day):** expect 60+ minutes from 11:00 onwards. Arriving before 10:45 is the only reliable move.

**TableCheck online (500 yen refundable cover, zero queue):**
- Visit DECOTTO's TableCheck page (searchable as "DECOTTO Ikebukuro TableCheck")
- English interface toggle at the top
- 30-minute slots from 11:00 onwards, **2 guests max per booking**
- Booking window opens 14 days before the date, closes at 17:00 the day before
- Cover charge shows up at checkout but is credited back against your food total at the venue

<div className="jpn-warning">
<strong>Heads up:</strong> As of Friday April 24 morning, the 11:30, 13:00 and 17:30 slots are still open for all 3 remaining days on TableCheck. After Sunday night everything closes — there is no extension planned and DECOTTO resumes normal sandwich service Monday April 27.
</div>

## Why Japanese People Love This

Ikebukuro's west gate has been the casual-female otaku anchor of Tokyo since the early 2000s — Otome Road, K-Books, BL specialty shops, and butler cafes define the strip. A diner-format MHA collab in this specific area, at this specific moment, lands differently than an Akihabara pop-up would. The local crowd treats it as a neighbourhood visit rather than a destination pilgrimage, which is why the weekday queues stay manageable even on short runs.

The 10th-anniversary timing also matters. Japanese readers first met My Hero Academia as a 2014 Weekly Shonen Jump debut; anyone who was a teenager then is now in their late 20s with disposable income and a waffle-sandwich price tolerance. DECOTTO's menu pricing at 1,380 to 1,480 yen — about 9 to 10 US dollars — sits exactly at the sweet spot Japanese office workers accept for a themed lunch during a short anniversary window. That is not an accident, and it is why the diner is running at capacity despite being a 24-day short run instead of a 2-month flagship.

The event design also respects the reader. No wristband, no photo-opp queue, no separate merch line — everything is handled through the main menu order, and the random coaster draws keep repeat customers coming back. It is the format DECOTTO has used across every recent licensed collab run, and the reason Ikebukuro regulars treat the diner as a dependable collab anchor rather than a one-time curiosity.
